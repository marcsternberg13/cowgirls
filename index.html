The feature documentary

The West was never just a man’s story.

These are the women painting a more complete picture.

Cowgirls follows contemporary female Western artists creating powerful, authentic work and building serious careers in galleries, museums, and private collections. The film celebrates their talent, their commercial success, and the cultural impact of artists who are changing how we see the American West.

Alongside the artists, the film hears from the gallery owners who represent them and the museum curators and art historians who are bringing their work into permanent collections. Red Steagall narrates in the voice of a campfire storyteller.

Painters, sculptors, ranchers, and businesswomen.

In three acts
I
Why now

Museums, collectors, and shows are turning toward women working in Western art.

II
The work

In the studio and on the land with artists who live the West they paint and sculpt.

III
The business

How these artists built careers, markets, and collector bases of their own.

Sit down with the artists.

Donna Howell-Sickles

Defined the modern cowgirl image in charcoal and acrylic. National Cowgirl Museum & Hall of Fame.

Terri Kelly Moyers

Among the most decorated painters working in the Western canon today.

Gladys Roldan-de-Moras

First Mexican-born woman inducted into the National Cowgirl Museum & Hall of Fame.

Shawn Cameron

Sixth-generation Arizona ranching family. First woman invited to exhibit with the Cowboy Artists of America.

Sally Maxwell

One of twelve artists recognized as a Master by the International Society of Scratchboard Artists.
